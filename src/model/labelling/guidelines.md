# Guidelines for Labeling

> prompt_version: 1.0 (Bump up on every change to the guidelines)

These guidelines are intended to help ensure consistency and accuracy in the labeling of support tickets.

## Quick Notes

Some tickets may have masked text, XXXX or redacted information, [REDACTED], or other placeholders. Please do not let
this affect your labeling decisions. The severity of the issue should be determined based on the information provided
in the ticket, regardless of any redactions.

## Levels of Severity

1. **Critical**: Issues that cause complete service outage or significant data loss. Immediate attention required.
   **Examples Include:** Fraud or identity theft happening now, account locked or funds frozen, foreclosure or
   repossession about to happen, legal threats.

2. **High**: Issues that significantly impact functionality or user experience but do not cause a complete outage.
   Prompt attention required. **Examples Include:** money already lost, a deadline coming up, repeated failed contact

3. **Medium**: Issues that affect functionality or user experience but do not require immediate attention. **Examples
   Include:** A wrong charge or reporting error with no immediate harm.

4. **Low**: Issues that have minimal impact on functionality or user experience. These can be addressed in due course.
   **Examples Include:** Minor UI glitches, feature requests, or general inquiries.

## Tie-Breaking Rules

When a ticket could reasonably be assigned to multiple severity levels, the following rules should be applied:

1. If the issue affects a large number of users, it should be assigned a higher severity level.
2. If the issue has a significant financial impact, it should be assigned a higher severity level.
3. If the issue is related to security or compliance, it should be assigned a higher severity level.
4. If the issue is urgent and requires immediate attention, it should be assigned a higher severity level.
